Automation

Triage classification & overflow routing

4-stage rule chain

The routing layer runs on a single-select Support Domain field with nine values — EMR systems, endpoint and MDM, identity and access, SaaS applications, hardware, security, major incident, service request, procurement. A four-stage automation chain writes it and acts on it: the triage agent classifies the domain on create, posts an impact and risk comment, routes on domain, then posts a resolution summary from worklogs and change history once the ticket closes.

Routing uses an overflow delegation model rather than round-robin — I hold every domain as the cross-domain administrator, and work only reaches the IAM, endpoint, EMR, or procurement specialist once my open count crosses a threshold. The automation encodes the real team structure instead of pretending the bench is flat.

  • Atlassian Rovo

Queues

Queue architecture

11 JQL-backed views

Eleven queues organized on three dimensions — criticality, operational domain, and lifecycle — each a JQL filter over the classification the automation produced. Major incident and security sort newest and highest-priority first; domain queues sort by priority then creation date so nothing ages out unseen; the hardware queue layers keyword detection over domain classification to catch tickets the classifier under-filled.

The design deliberately separates routing from visibility: automation owns assignment, queues are a read layer. Nobody sorts a queue to decide who works what.
