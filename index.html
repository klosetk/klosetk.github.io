Automation

Triage classification & overflow routing

4-stage rule chain

Routing runs on a single-select Support Domain field with nine values: EMR systems, endpoint and MDM, identity and access, SaaS applications, hardware, security, major incident, service request, and procurement. A chain of automation rules writes and acts on it: the triage agent classifies the domain on create, posts an impact and risk comment, routes on domain, and posts a resolution summary from worklogs and change history once the ticket closes.

Routing uses an overflow delegation model rather than round-robin. I hold every domain as the cross-domain administrator, and work reaches the IAM, endpoint, EMR, or procurement specialist only once my open count crosses a threshold. The automation encodes the real team structure instead of assuming a flat bench.

  • Atlassian Rovo

Queues

Queue architecture

11 JQL-backed views

Queues are organized on three dimensions (criticality, operational domain, and lifecycle), each a JQL filter over the classification the automation produced. Major incident and security sort newest and highest-priority first. Domain queues sort by priority, then creation date, so nothing ages out unseen. The hardware queue layers keyword detection over domain classification to catch tickets the classifier under-filled.

The design separates routing from visibility: automation owns assignment, and queues are a read layer, so nobody sorts a queue to decide who works what.
